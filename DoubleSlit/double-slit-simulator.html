Horizontal axis: electron transverse position y at the detection screen. Vertical axis: pointer-particle position z. Before the slit, Ψ factorizes as ψe(y)·φ0(z) — a single lump near z = 0. After the slit with measurement ON, the pointer splits into two lumps moving in opposite z directions, each entangled with one slit-path. As the pointer wave packets separate, their overlap ⟨φup|φdn⟩ drops to zero — the marginal electron distribution along y loses its fringes. This is decoherence in a single-particle bath.

Setup. A coherent electron Gaussian packet approaches a barrier with two narrow slits. Downstream lies a detection screen. A single pointer particle lives in an extra dimension z, coupled to the slit when the Observe toggle is ON: it is nudged to +z if the electron went through the upper slit, to −z if it went through the lower slit.

No observation. The joint state stays factorable: Ψ(y, z, t) = ψe(y, t)·φ0(z). The slit amplitudes ψup + ψdn add coherently: |ψup + ψdn|² shows the classic interference fringes on the screen.

With observation. After the electron traverses the slit, the state is entangled: Ψ = ψup·φup + ψdn·φdn. The displayed screen probability traces over the pointer: |ψup|² + |ψdn|² + 2·⟨φup|φdn⟩·Re(ψup*ψdn). The pointer overlap decays as a Gaussian of the pointer separation, and the cross term — the interference — vanishes. Which-path information now lives in the pointer.

Physics is stylized for real-time rendering: free-particle Gaussian wave packets with pragmatic spreading, not a finite-difference Schrödinger solve. But the qualitative features — fringes, diffraction envelope, which-path decoherence, entanglement structure in configuration space — are faithful.
